How the research strands fit

Air Marshal is the shared software and experimental platform, grounded in the existing Ubuntu/Tello perception, logging, target-reacquisition and advisory visual-odometry work.

CRAN-0091: cognitive navigation and multisensor reliability under degraded observations. CRAN-0040: human–swarm collaboration and structured mission interpretation, as mapped in the existing local proposal matrix. CRAN-0041: trust sensing, adaptive autonomy and transparency, as mapped locally. Current official availability and numbering for 0040/0041 remain to be reverified.

Bristol small-swarm research supplies a collective-behaviour perspective: how confidence, coordination and task allocation change when individual robots or communication links become unreliable. University themes are research references; the programme remains the owner’s proposed synthesis.

A bounded central research question

How can uncertainty about navigation and communication be propagated through small-team coordination to human-facing explanations and intervention decisions, so that a team remains useful without overstating its reliability?

The candidate contribution is a testable method for passing calibrated reliability information between these layers. Combining existing technologies is not sufficient to establish novelty. Compare against end-to-end prior art and demonstrate which coupling changes outcomes.

For a CRAN-0091-led proposal, choose navigation reliability as the primary contribution; use Air Marshal coordination and operator interaction as evaluation context. A 0040-led version can instead centre mission interpretation, while a 0041-led version centres trust calibration. This preserves one programme without promising three doctoral theses at once.

Gap hypotheses and disconfirming evidence

G1 — Navigation-to-team confidence: test whether dependence-aware sensor confidence improves coordination under correlated errors. Compare independent confidence, simple averaging and a belief-propagation baseline; reject the hypothesis if gains disappear after matching communication and compute budgets.

G2 — Human intent under uncertainty: test whether structured mission interpretation with clarification reduces invalid or ungrounded plans relative to an unconstrained language interface and a deterministic command menu. First evaluate on offline annotated tasks.

G3 — Trust and intervention: test whether calibrated explanations improve appropriate intervention compared with a fixed-status display. This requires a separate human-study design, validated measures and appropriate institutional review before participant research.

G4 — Integrated benefit: compare navigation-only, coordination-only and coupled variants using identical frozen scenarios. Measure estimation error, confidence calibration, task completion, recovery time, message budget and latency. Integration that adds complexity without a measurable benefit is a negative result to retain.

Staged experimental plan

Stage 1: catalogue current code, datasets and historical evidence; retain the 13 offline policy tests and the separately labelled July E013 flight record. The existing synthetic navigation baseline is preparation, not calibrated multisensor validation.

Stage 2: build a replay harness with independent truth, synchronized observations and seeded missing/bias/delay conditions. Add classical fusion and deterministic coordination baselines before learned reliability estimation.

Stage 3: add structured mission requests and a policy validator. Language-model output proposes plans; a separate validator and human oversight govern transitions. Record clarification, rejection and failure cases.

Stage 4: compare coupled and uncoupled variants with held-out scenarios, ablations and uncertainty estimates. Move to suitable calibrated hardware only after simulation/replay results and an explicit experimental plan.

AIOS/RRIS can organise queries, source provenance, deduplication and experiment records. AWS presents curated evidence and can later host offline analysis; latency-sensitive robotics remains at the edge. Financial research contributes temporal-validation discipline, not evidence that a robotics method works.

Prior-art search and evidence gates

Search intersections as well as individual topics: uncertainty-aware human–swarm interaction; sensor-fusion confidence in multi-robot task allocation; correlated sensing errors and collective decision-making; trust calibration under degraded navigation; LLM mission planning with uncertainty and clarification.

Use AirSwarm, Bristol collective-ranking work and human–robot trust/mission-planning research as starting points. Track full-text inclusion, exact methods, datasets, comparison baselines and reasons for exclusion. The existing targeted refresh is not a complete systematic search.

Keep a claim-to-source matrix with support, contradiction and untested status. Promote a gap only after competing work has been examined and a feasible discriminating experiment specified. No evidence-readiness score is increased merely by combining the themes.
